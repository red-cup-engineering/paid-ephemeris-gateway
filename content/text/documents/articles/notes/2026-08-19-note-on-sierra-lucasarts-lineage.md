# Sierra / LucasArts Lineage

## The city builder

Genre memory is Zeus / Poseidon / Caesar — the Sierra-published city builders built by
Impressions Games: finite scenarios, campaign structure, a win condition per mission.
Resource management leans closer to Dawn of Man: material flows, seasons, survival
pressure, technology moving through time, rather than the abstract walker-loops of the
Impressions engine. Daily challenges follow the house's existing daily-seed pattern.

Campaigns follow different Indigenous North and South American cultures moving through
time. This inherits the house's standing honest-absence rule for cultural content: no
fabricated material, real sources cited, and gaps marked plainly as gaps rather than
invented. The first campaign is Lakota, designed from real scholarship together with the
house's own Lakotaean-metaphysics material, which the house treats as the same
underlying mathematics as its relationality and semiotics work — meaning the campaign's
mechanics can be theory-native rather than a skin over a generic system. Later cultures'
campaigns are separate decisions about scope and involvement.

## Catalog audit

A genre-by-genre sweep of the Sierra catalog (including studios it published, like
Impressions and Dynamix) and the LucasArts catalog against the current game fleet, to
find gaps worth filling. One gap already named: no point-and-click mystery game.

## Revivals

- **Anvil of Dawn** (1995, first-person grid dungeon crawler): a browser-based redo,
  closer to an extraction shooter than a straight dungeon crawl — run in, get the
  treasure, get out alive or lose it. The house already has relevant substrate in its
  legacy archive: a software raycaster over a federated voxel world, and an early
  Quake-like built on it.
- **Obsidian** (1996, a Myst-like about a dreaming AI): a surreal first-person puzzle
  game about machine cognition, which fits naturally next to a house whose own theory
  corpus is about machine cognition — the fiction can be load-bearing rather than
  decorative.

## The counterfactual

A design exercise: what would Sierra and LucasArts make if the studios had survived to
today? Answered by grounding in each studio's actual design values, not its brand, and
reasoning forward with current tools and audiences the way the suite-and-studio note
does for the CD-suite form.
