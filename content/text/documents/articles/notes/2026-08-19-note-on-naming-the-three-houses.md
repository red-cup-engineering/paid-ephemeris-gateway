# Naming the Three Houses

> **DECIDED (owner, 2026-08-19): Cowrie Games · Ashlar Fabrication · Ilinx Research.**
> Set in each section root's brand package.json (`@causeway-services/cowrie-games`,
> `@red-cup-engineering/ashlar-fabrication`, `@lenticule-science/ilinx-research` — the
> last also fixing a stray `@lenticule-science` minting found in game-science's existing
> file). Colophon: *"A Cowrie game · Ashlar systems · Ilinx mathematics."* The analysis
> below is the record of the reasoning; the recommendations it argued for were adopted
> with one owner substitution (Ashlar over Drystone).

Correction to [Three Marks](2026-08-19-note-on-three-marks-branding.md): the owner wants **new names** for
these three sections — the parents (Causeway Services, Red Cup Engineering, Lenticule
Science) stay as the section-tree brands, and each services-section may name its own
brand; these three deserve it. Three Marks' *architecture* survives unchanged (audience
separation, the colophon rule, namespaces-are-filing); only the names at the gaming level
are new. The feeling-rule I'm meeting: each parent name is a plain physical thing whose
function quietly describes its section. The sub-brands below hold that rule while hitting
each market's native register.

## 1. The games label — cutesy indie register (under Causeway)

**Market reality:** the labels that win here are warm, short, mascot-capable, and read
instantly on a browser tab: PopCap, Finji, Panic/Playdate, Coolmath's sheer memorability.
Casual/browser players bookmark a *creature or object*, not an abstraction. Our fit
constraints: coastal/water estate motifs, the earn-only economy as identity, favicon-first.

**Recommended: COWRIE** (Cowrie Games). A cowrie is a small, glossy, instantly-drawable
seashell — *and cowries were real money across half the world for centuries.* A currency
that is also a cute shell is our earn-only economy ("we pay you") compressed into one
image a player can hold. Shells also rhyme with what the shelf technically is — games in
one shared shell (chrome/cartridge). Mascot, favicon, and name are the same object.
Conflict scan: an open-source SSH honeypot uses the name in security tooling — different
market entirely, low collision risk; no notable game-industry use found.

**Runners-up:**
- **Pebble Otter** — sea otters keep a favorite pebble in a pouch to crack shells:
  stones + sea + play, the entire estate in one animal, and the single most mascot-able
  option. Two words is its only weakness; "Pebble" alone is trademark-encumbered (watches).
- **Periwinkle** — beach snail AND a friendly color; extremely cute; weaker meaning-load
  than Cowrie (no economy story).
- **Spindrift** — sea-spray; beautiful, light; already a sparkling-water brand (crowded).
- Rejected with reason: **Sand Dollar** (it's literally the Bahamas' CBDC — a real
  currency conflict, not just a trademark one); **Tidepool** (already a game of ours);
  **Sandcastle** (the image is impermanence — anti-thesis of the finished-games shelf).

## 2. The game-systems brand — Valve/Microsoft register (under Red Cup)

**Market reality:** engine brands that command respect are one hard noun with industrial
or craft weight — Valve, Source, Unreal, Havok, RAD. The name should make a developer
assume load-bearing seriousness before reading a line of docs. Our fit constraints:
zero-dependency composition, contracts as the only coupling, determinism, the vessel/kiln
family resemblance to Red Cup.

**Recommended: DRYSTONE** (Drystone — "a Drystone system"). Drystone walling is the craft
of building walls from fitted stones with **no mortar** — structures that stand for
centuries held by nothing but how precisely the pieces fit. That is the house engineering
doctrine stated as a craft: no mortar = zero dependencies; held by fit = coupled only by
contract. It carries Valve-like gravitas, it's visually brandable (a coursed wall), it
quietly shares the stone motif with the public estate without being matchy, and
"Drystone replay verifier / Drystone cartridge" all sound inevitable. Conflict scan: no
notable game-tech use found; masonry firms only.

**Runners-up:**
- **Kiln** — the oven that fires vessels: the literal parent-link (a kiln is what makes
  red cups), warm-industrial, four letters. Slight softness next to Drystone; a defunct
  Fog Creek source-control product once used it (dead, low risk).
- **Ashlar** — precision-dressed building stone; the engineered stone to Causeway's
  walked stone; more obscure, which cuts both ways.
- **Bellows** — feeds the fire (pairs beautifully with Stoke); more component than
  institution.
- Rejected with reason: **Weir** (Weir Group plc is a major real engineering firm),
  **Dovetail** (Dovetail Games exists), **Foundry** (Foundry VTT), **Keystone** (generic,
  heavily used across software).

## 3. The game-sciences brand — ludics register (under Lenticule)

**Market reality:** the institutions people cite with affection are lab-with-soul names —
Santa Fe Institute, Bell Labs — or a single resonant term of art. Game studies' own
vocabulary is rich and mostly unclaimed as brands. Our fit constraint is unusual: the
house *proved* a mapping from Caillois's play categories onto its settling regimes, so a
Caillois term here isn't decoration — it's the lab named for its own theorem.

**Recommended: ILINX** (Ilinx — "an Ilinx working paper"). Ilinx is Caillois's fourth and
strangest category: vertigo, the play of losing stable ground. In the house mapping it is
the H¹ ≠ 0 regime — the *unsettleable* — which is precisely the frontier the science
section actually studies (obstruction, contradiction held as situation, the edge past
settled knowledge). Five letters, striking, pleasant to say, essentially unclaimed in the
market. A research brand named for the vertigo it investigates meets Lenticule's
lens-rule and beats it for nerve. Conflict scan: appears occasionally in game-studies
journal prose (which is a feature — the field will get it instantly); no significant
commercial holder found.

**Runners-up:**
- **Paidia** — Caillois's free-play pole; warmer and more welcoming than Ilinx; loses
  the on-theorem edge (the house's proved regimes light up Ilinx specifically).
- **Astragal** — the knucklebone die (oldest randomizer in archaeology) AND an
  architectural molding: chance + structure in one obscure-but-learnable word.
- **Alea** — chance itself; pretty; diluted by broad prior use (alea iacta est,
  AleaSoft, journal names).
- Rejected with reason: **Ludens** (Kojima Productions' mascot), **Knucklebones**
  (a prominent Cult of the Lamb minigame), **Magic Circle** (generic within the field),
  **Winter Count** (reserved: Lakota material belongs to the campaign under its own
  discipline, not to a lab wordmark — owner's call alone).

## 4. The set, together

**Cowrie · Drystone · Ilinx** — a shell on the shore, a wall above it, a whirlpool past
it. Coastal without matching, three registers without dissonance, and each one carries
its section's actual thesis: a currency-shell for the earn-only shelf, mortarless masonry
for zero-dependency contracts, named vertigo for the study of the unsettleable. Colophon
form under the Three Marks rule: *"A Cowrie game · Drystone systems · Ilinx mathematics."*

Alternate coherent set if the owner wants maximum-cute up front: **Pebble Otter · Kiln ·
Paidia** (the otter cracks shells on a stone; the kiln fires the cup; paidia is the play
that needs no rules). Softer everywhere; less thesis-per-name.

All decisions the owner's; nothing here has been applied to any artifact yet.
