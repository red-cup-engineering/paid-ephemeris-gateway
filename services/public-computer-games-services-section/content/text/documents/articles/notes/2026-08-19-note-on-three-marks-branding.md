The owner asked (2026-08-19) for a reasoning pass on branding the public gaming services
section, the internal game engineering section, and the internal game sciences section.
The finding up front: **we don't need to invent brands — we need to notice the ones the
tree already carries, because all three names accidentally-or-not describe their section's
actual function.** Branding here is the same move as the filing correction: stop inventing,
start reading what's there.

## 0. Why three brands and not one

One brand would collapse three audiences with incompatible needs. A player needs to trust
us fast; a builder needs contracts and composability; a reader of the theory needs
epistemic rigor. The Hoare × Rendanheyi doctrine already separates these as layers —
zero-distance user membrane, systems-by-contract, evidence — so the brand architecture is
just that doctrine made legible:

| Audience | Brand | Section | Namespace |
|---|---|---|---|
| Players | **Causeway** | public games (this section) | `@causeway-services` |
| Builders (human + agent) | **Red Cup** | game systems (engineering) | `@red-cup-engineering` |
| Readers / the field | **Lenticule** | game sciences (science) | `@lenticule-science` |

## 1. Causeway — the shelf players walk

**The image:** a causeway is stones laid across water so people can cross. It is public
infrastructure, walkable by anyone, built one stone at a time, and it connects islands.

**Why it's almost overdetermined for this catalog:** the house's games are stones —
*onestone*, *Farstone*, weiqi's stones on the board — and the destination the whole
staircase walks toward is water: Tidepool, the undersea MUD, the SEA of the SHRIMP
ecology, the Deep shelf. A causeway is exactly what play.561.group is: a laid path of
individual stones (games) that together let a stranger cross into our water. The CD-suite
thesis (2026-08-19-note-on-the-suite-and-the-studio.md) gets a name: the shelf is the causeway.

**What the Causeway brand owns:** the shelf chrome (the modern card back), the Today page,
the trust copy — and the trust copy IS the brand: *"We sell ads and share the revenue with
the players. We never take your money to play, never let you wager it, and never pay out
more than we verifiably earned."* No wagering, no energy walls, published odds, earn-only.
Causeway is the only brand players ever need to see.

**What Causeway must never do:** ship a dark pattern (the executable-ethics tests are the
brand's enforcement arm); brand a system or a theory doc (those aren't its audience);
promise what the ledger can't prove.

**Voice:** warm, plain, a little wry — Kingdom-of-Loathing-adjacent humor is in-bounds on
the shelf (the Chutes & Ladders bot IS a die, and we say so). Never solemn, never salesy.

**Proposed marks:** wordmark "Causeway" with per-game titles as the stones ("a Causeway
game"). Games keep their own names and palettes — same card back, different felt.

## 2. Red Cup — the vessel that holds anything

**The image:** the red cup is the plainest vessel there is — sturdy, ubiquitous,
unembarrassed, and it holds whatever you pour into it. That is precisely what a game
*system* is under the house doctrine: a contract-shaped container indifferent to its
contents. The cartridge package is literally this image as software.

**What the Red Cup brand owns:** the systems under computer-game-system-services-section
(cartridge, energy-bank, parlor-kernel, vault-compiler, the render family) plus the wider
engineering substrate (feltos, qwzspace, the ActivityPub server). Its brand artifacts are
READMEs, contracts, and invariants — the org chart that is a test suite.

**A real naming convention worth canonizing (it already exists):** Red Cup names its
*engines* after pioneers — Abrash (software 3D), Tsander (rocketry → wireframe engine),
Wheatstone (telegraphy → MUD client), Daleske, Arnold. Proposal: keep this as the house
rule — **engines carry pioneer names; packages carry plain nouns** (cartridge,
energy-bank, parlor-kernel). The pioneer name says "this is a deep system with a lineage";
the plain noun says "this is a vessel, use it." Both are Red Cup.

**What Red Cup must never do:** appear on a player-facing surface (the chrome is
Causeway's; a player should be able to love the shelf without ever learning the plumbing's
name); claim a theory result (cite Lenticule instead); publish a package whose README
states more than is true — the brand's reputation is exactly the reliability of its
contracts.

**Voice:** terse, precise, load-bearing. A Red Cup README reads like a datasheet with dry
wit permitted in comments.

## 3. Lenticule — the small lens

**The image:** a lenticule is a small lens — and an array of lenticules is how one image
shows differently from different angles. The house's deepest standing claim is exactly
this: *Obsmetrologia = relationality = semiotics = Lakotaean metaphysics — four lenses on
one mathematics.* The science brand's name already IS the doctrine.

**What the Lenticule brand owns:** game-science-services-section — ludics (both senses:
Caillois's play categories and Girard's ludics, and the house holds a real
Girard–Caillois correspondence note), the market teardowns, the regime taxonomy, the
theory bindings that every ARCHITECTURE.md §13 cites, weiqi's kernel-as-executable-
glossary program, the Lakota campaign sourcebook discipline. Proposed program name for
the game-study work specifically: **Lenticule Ludics**.

**What Lenticule must never do:** decorate. Its brand promise is the house's evidentiary
standard — recurrence-cited, honest-absence-preserving, "state only what is true." A
Lenticule citation in a game's design doc means *this mechanic is real mathematics*; the
brand dies the first time that's false. (The Equivo/vendor-a-copy overclaim a verifier once
caught is the exact failure mode; the correction discipline is the brand.)

**Voice:** the primary-source voice the theory corpus already has. No press-release
register, ever.

## 4. How the three marks touch

- **The colophon rule (proposal):** every shipped game may carry one quiet line on its
  about/credits surface — *"A Causeway game · Red Cup systems · Lenticule mathematics"* —
  and that is the ONLY place the three marks co-appear. One line, load-ordered by
  audience distance.
- **§13 Theory Binding cites Lenticule; package deps cite Red Cup; trust copy cites
  Causeway.** No crossing: a spec never markets, a shelf page never proves theorems.
- **Namespaces are the brands** (vision note §5b): the npm scope a package lives in is a
  branding decision already made by filing it correctly. Branding follows filing — which
  is why today's refile had to happen before this note could.
- **teraum is outside all three marks**, permanently.

## 5. Open choices left to the owner (decided nothing here)

1. Whether "Causeway" surfaces as a wordmark on the shelf now, or after the Cloudflare
   domain pass (a brand debut alongside real URLs is stronger).
2. Whether the pioneer-name convention extends backward (renaming nothing existing —
   only whether NEW engines must follow it).
3. Whether "Lenticule Ludics" is the program's public name or stays internal.
4. Wordmark/visual design for all three — deliberately unproposed; the shelf chrome's
   tokens are the only visual identity work done so far, and it is Causeway's.
