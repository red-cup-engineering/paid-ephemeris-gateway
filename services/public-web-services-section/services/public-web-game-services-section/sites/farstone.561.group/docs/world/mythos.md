# The Mythos of Farstone

This file is the deep canon under the municipal canon: where the island
actually sits, why it is cold, what the founders knew, what the crafts
mean, and who carries which piece of the island's story.
The sibling files hold the map ([`geography.md`](./geography.md)), the
living island ([`biome.md`](./biome.md)), the cast
([`inhabitants.md`](./inhabitants.md)), the tone laws
([`lore.md`](./lore.md)), and the stuff ([`things.md`](./things.md));
this file holds the island's soul.
Like everything in this directory it is **data, not code**, written to
survive every engine rewrite.
The lore here is a carrier, not cargo: each piece of it exists to make a
simulation system legible, and the systems are designed in
[`../SYSTEMS.md`](../SYSTEMS.md).

Where an element here is derived from the 561 Group's own theory
carriers, the carrier is cited by repository path in backticks.
The derivation is deliberate: the theory breathes through the world as
craft, weather, and proverb — never as jargon in an NPC's mouth.

## Where the island sits

Farstone is a granite island in the **cold North Atlantic**, the largest
of the Farr Archipelago, on the shelf off a cool maritime coast.
The water around it is cold the year round and the winters are real:
the biome record allows the island only ~150 frost-free days, which
leaves two hundred and more that can bite.
Snow lies on the quay most winters; the stove is the center of every
indoor room from Slackwater to thaw.

The sea's cold is not a hardship bolted onto the setting — it is the
setting.
Everything distinctive about Farstone (the Off-Season, the stove-circles,
the felting months, the Reefs) follows from cold water and short days.

Offshore, the cold is productive.
The banks north of the harbor mouth carry **stone-coral reefs and
glass-sponge grounds** — reefs of cold, dark water, not warm lagoons.
This is honest North Atlantic marine truth: the real ocean grows its
largest coral reefs in cold deep water off Norway (the Røst Reef, inside
the Arctic Circle) and mounds of the same stone-coral off Scotland, with
kelp forests and horse-mussel beds inshore of them.
Farstone's Reefs are canon in exactly that register: a cold fishing
bank, a snag-ground, and a graveyard of keels — never a tropical lagoon.

## The tide is the clock

Farstone keeps time by water, not by the hour.
The tides are semidiurnal — two high waters a day — and islanders fix
appointments to them: *on the flood*, *at slack*, *when the bar covers*.
The harbormaster's barograph and the tide-table shelf in the library
(a century deep) are the island's true civic instruments.
The year is quartered the same way the day is halved: spring, summer,
autumn, winter, and the town's whole economy breathes in that cycle.

And the clock never stops.
Farstone has no final position, no reset, no clean ending behind which
anything can be safely confined; harm and friendship both accumulate,
which is why the islanders are so careful about both.
(This is the persistent-world law the house glossary states plainly —
"persistence is what removes a game's exit" — at
`lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/engineering-services-section/services/computer-engineering-services-section/services/computer-systems-engineering-services-section/services/semiotic-computer-system-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/persistent-world.md`;
the island is a world, not a game, and it acts like one.)

## The four eras

Farstone's history runs in four eras.
The first two live in stone and story; the second two live in the
records office.
The municipal dates in [`lore.md`](./lore.md) (1872, 1923, 1987, 2024)
all fall inside the last two.

**1. The Given Age** *(about four hundred years ago).*
The founders crossed cold water for the fishing bank and stayed for the
harbor.
Their first lord is the one the worn cemetery stones still name — the
man in the green-veined sarcophagus under the chapel, buried with a
grey-iron blade and a bronze torc.
They cut catacombs into the living rock before they raised any chapel,
and deeper than their dead they found what the island stands on: the
warm spring, and below it a drowned vault older than themselves.
They gave to it.
The Stair of Shells — every step paved with pressed offerings — is
their liturgy fossilized.
The oldest island saying is theirs: **the sea keeps what it is given.**

**2. The Chapel Age.**
The town grew and grew respectable.
The chapel was raised over the founders' dead, and the thing in the deep
was given a tame granite face and a gentle name: Maraset, the grey-eyed
lady of tide and harbor.
Wool and felt became the landward half of the economy; the Mariners'
Guild, the Saltloft, and the almshouse date to this era, and so do the
fulling-stocks whose successors still thump in the wool-walk every
winter.

**3. The Quarry Years.**
The prosperous era, and the proud one: South Point Light raised in 1872,
the Strake quarry stepped into the mountain's flank, the iron seam
chased below it, the charter adopted in 1923, the light automated in
'87.
It ended the way pride ends on Farstone — the mine took a cave-in about
thirty years ago, the survey below the third level stops mid-line, and
the adit was barricaded.
The quarry still works stone in season; nobody works the dark under it.

**4. The Lean Years** *(now).*
The catch thins, the summer trade goes home earlier each year, half the
fleet ties up for the winter, and the 2024 storm cracked the
breakwater's western face.
The dredging permit — Application 23, stamped both *within-window* and
*overdue* by two hands that did not speak — is this era's signature: a
town pressed hard enough that its contradictions stand out like grain
in scoured wood.
This is the era the player lands in.
Its name, on the island as in the engine, is **the Off-Season**, and on
a North Atlantic island the off-season is the winter.

## Felt: the founding craft-metaphysic

The island's oldest craft is felting, and felting is how Farstone
understands itself.

Felt is not woven.
No thread in it follows a plan; wool locks because each fiber is scaled
in one direction, and under water, heat, and agitation the fibers
migrate, tangle, and can never come back out — an **irreversible
contact-locking of directed fibers**, which is why a good felt boot
outlasts the boat it ships on.
(The mechanism is real and the house has studied it as mathematics:
`lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/06/26/keratin_mathematical_material_object_survey.md`,
§6 — felting as irreversible contact-locking in a population of directed
scale-covered rods.)

The islanders' material sermon, heard in the fulling-shed every winter:
**nothing in felt agrees with anything else, and that is why it holds.**
A woven cloth fails along its own order; felt has no order to fail
along.

The same sermon is in the island's stone.
Quarrymen on Farstone grade granite the way jade-workers grade jade:
not by hardness but by **toughness**, and toughness lives in the grain
not agreeing.
Nephrite jade is literally *felted stone* — a felted mass of
interlocking fibers with no shared orientation — and it is tough
precisely because of the permanent, load-bearing misalignment at every
grain boundary; a single crystal has one cleavage plane and fails
catastrophically along it, and glass, with no grain at all, fails by
unbounded pathless fracture.
(The house carrier that states this three-regime law is the JADE
glossary term,
`lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/engineering-services-section/content/text/glossary/terms/jacobian-arena-difference-engine.md`.)

This is the metaphysic under the town's signature mechanic.
Farstone *holds its contradictions* — the hydrant passed and failed, the
permit within-window and overdue — because the island's own crafts
taught it that a held disagreement is a grain boundary, and grain
boundaries are where toughness lives.
A town that resolved every dispute would be a single crystal: one
cleavage plane, one bad winter, gone.
Farstone is felt.
**Tough, not hard** is the island's verdict on everything it approves
of — boats, boots, agreements, people.

## Glass, beads, and the lattice

Glass is felt's opposite pole, and the islanders treat it accordingly:
beautiful, useful, and not to be trusted bare.
The light's lens, the glass floats the sea gives back on the
wrack-line, Pip Fizzle's absurd fizzing spring bottled in it — glass on
Farstone is always either watched (the light is *tended*, even
automated) or **beaded**: strung, netted, knotted into something with
grain.
A bare pane shatters; a beaded float rides out a gale in a net bag.
The bead-string and the net are the same craft at two scales — rigid
elements locked into a flexible web until the web itself bears load.
(The house has this as mathematics too: beading transforms flexible
fiber networks into load-bearing structures —
`lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/06/26/2026-06-26-survey-of-beadwork-mathematics.md` —
and as a craft of pattern that never repeats,
`lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/public-services-section/services/personal-services-section/emsenn-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/06/27/2026-06-27-beadwork-as-finite-observable-of-aperiodic-order.md`.)
Morrigan's mussel-shell charms, the fishwives' bead-strung creels, and
the trade-beads in Riba Voss's ledger all belong to this register.

The finest thing the cold sea sends up is the third way between glass
and felt: **sponge-lace**.
Off the sponge-ground beyond the Reefs, a deep haul sometimes brings up
the skeleton of a glass sponge — a white lattice tube, glass to the
touch, built of crossed and diagonal struts so that a crack starting
anywhere is arrested at the next joint, and holding, in the old ones, a
paired set of shrimp husks: two creatures that entered young, grew, and
lived out their lives inside the lattice.
Islanders mount sponge-lace over the hearth and give it at weddings,
and the toast that goes with it is the whole island's theory of a good
house: *a lattice that stops its cracks and keeps a pair alive inside.*
(The living original is *Euplectella*, the Venus' flower-basket, and the
house's deepest engineering note is built on exactly this skeleton —
crack arrest, diagonal load-sharing, the breeding pair in the cavity:
`lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/engineering-services-section/content/text/documents/articles/notes/2026/08/01/2026-08-01-note-on-the-content-addressed-glass-sponge-semantic-compute-complex.md`,
§14.3.)

## Maraset, the grey-eyed lady

The chapel's Maraset is the island's tutelary lady: grey-eyed, patient,
keeper of the drowned.
Her worship is quiet — tallow light, the green seaward window, the
chapel-quiet that is a balm against the off-season — and her law is the
founders' law with the blood rinsed off: **give the sea its share.**
First catch of the season over the side; a shell pressed into the
harbor mud when a crew comes home whole; the drowned never spoken of as
*lost*, always as *kept*.
The children add that the grey lady walks the shingle strand when
somebody is going to drown, and no adult on the island will say out
loud that they don't believe it.

What the chapel worships and what sleeps in the drowned vault under the
island are, by every test the world can apply, the same — and the
island **holds that contradiction too**.
Father Coll keeps the tame reading; Morrigan keeps the wild one; Miss
Ivy Renn keeps the shelf of marsh-lore the chapel would rather was
burned; Old Senan keeps the chained vault-door and his own counsel.
None of them is written to be right.
The parish's deepest fact is a ⊤ cell, exactly like the permit's, and
the canon law of [`lore.md`](./lore.md) applies in full: never resolve
it.

## The Reefs

North of the fishing grounds the bottom rises to the **Reefs**: banks of
cold-water stone-coral standing up out of dark water, kelp on the
shallow crowns, horse-mussel beds in the lee, and fish thick around all
of it in season — the reason the founders came.
The Reefs are the richest water Farstone has and the most expensive:
coral snags nets, the passes shift with the sand, and the crowns break
the swell in a blow.
The island's wreck-registers are mostly Reef entries, and the newest
mast still standing out there belongs to the *Gannet*, hard on the
crown these many winters, stripped by every generation of salvors since.

Working the Reefs is pilotage: knowing the passes, the marks, and the
tide that covers the crowns.
The trade's names are honest rigging vocabulary both ways — you *reef*
a sail coming home from the Reefs in the weather that makes them
dangerous — and the reef-pilots wear the pun as a surname without
smiling about it.

## The factions, in flesh

Four factions hold the island between them.
They are real allegiances, not uniforms; every named islander leans on
one, and the engine's standing, tension, and demand systems read them.

- **The Fleet** (`fishers`) — the working water-side: quay, market row,
  net-loft, boatyard, guild hall, and the wool-walk (the Fleet takes in
  every working trade, landward wool included, because the wool pays
  for the winter the fish no longer do).
  Creed: *the catch is shared work; the sea settles debts.*
  Orla and Yorrick Vane, Greer Tenn, Wenna Drust, Dax Pell, Coilin Vey,
  Brannaugh Ross, Reef Halyard, Bett Harrow.
- **The Watch** (`watch`) — the civic register: constable, harbormaster,
  records clerk, fire chief.
  Creed: *the charter holds when the weather doesn't.*
  Mara Quill, Harbormaster Quist, Clerk Halloran, Chief Bram Oakes.
- **The Chapel** (`chapel`) — the keepers of quiet and of the record of
  the dead: Father Coll, Sister Edda, Miss Ivy Renn, Old Senan,
  Schoolmistress Vell, Old Pell at his lamp.
  Creed: *give the sea its share, and keep the names.*
- **The Marsh** (`marsh`) — the outside edge, where the island's map of
  what is proper runs out: Morrigan, Pip Fizzle, Cael the Collier, Riba
  Voss.
  Creed, so far as it has one: *the island was here before the town.*

The standing tensions: Fleet against Watch over the permit and the
tied-up winter; Chapel against Marsh over which Maraset is the real
one; Fleet against Marsh wherever Riba Voss's clean coin touches the
quay; Watch against Marsh on principle and without much energy.
Scarcity sharpens all four edges every winter — that is the drama
engine, per the ludics-design reading in
[`inhabitants.md`](./inhabitants.md), and none of it is scripted.

## The load-bearing cast

Twelve islanders carry the mythos on their backs.
Dispositions and stakes are the canon; dialogue never is.

1. **Wenna Drust, the net-mender.**
   The island's memory at working speed: news reaches the net-loft a
   day before it happens.
   She is also the keeper of the felt sermon — she says *nothing in
   felt agrees, that's why it holds* the way the chapel says grace —
   and the young fleet hands absorb their island metaphysics from her
   needle-side bench without noticing.
2. **Chief Bram Oakes, the fire chief.**
   Reads the dry-spell board like scripture in summer and spends the
   winter sharpening hooks against the spark that mustn't come.
   Off-season he is the Watch's warm face: the one civic officer the
   Fleet will still drink with, which makes him the island's last
   working hinge when the permit fight jams the rest.
3. **Nessa Cawl, the innkeeper.**
   Warmth against the cold, not abundance — the stew-pot, the beds, the
   fire.
   The Saltloft is the biggest stove-circle on the island and Nessa is
   its law: no feud crosses her threshold, which is why every feud
   negotiates there.
4. **Yorrick Vane, the chandler** (and Guildmaster Orla, her sister).
   The commercial establishment, stern and solvent.
   Between them the sisters hold the Fleet's purse and its papers, and
   they have not fully agreed on anything since childhood — the Fleet's
   own internal grain boundary, load-bearing like the rest.
5. **Greer Tenn, the fishwife.**
   Loud, shrewd, broad-armed; sells the thin catch and knows everyone's
   business.
   Between her stall and Wenna's loft, the island has two rival news
   services and no secrets.
6. **Dax Pell, the out-of-work deckhand.**
   The winter's pressure made a person: hungry, restless, spoiling.
   The player's first mirror — feed him, talk him down, or fight him —
   and the season's mood made flesh.
7. **Sergeant Mara Quill, the harbor constable.**
   Steady, fair, twenty years on the quay, and rival to Dax through no
   plan of her own — the quay's short fuse is the Watch and the Fleet
   standing too close together all winter.
8. **Clerk Halloran, the records clerk.**
   The quiet center of the Lean Years: holds Application 23 in two
   minds at once and will not force it, because forcing it is not his
   office.
   The town thinks him weak; the canon knows he is the only man on the
   island doing the signature thing — holding the contradiction —
   on purpose, in public, as a duty.
9. **Harbormaster Quist.**
   Square, unhurried; opens and closes the harbor on the word of a
   falling glass.
   When the storm-cone goes up the whole mythos contracts to her
   window: the tide is the clock, and she is its hand.
10. **Morrigan, the marsh-witch.**
    Keeps the wild reading of Maraset, the cures the chapel frowns on,
    and the paths past the salt marsh.
    The Marsh's whole creed in one person; the chapel's necessary
    opposite; buyer of luminous things nobody should bring up from
    below.
11. **Riba Voss, the fence of the sea-caves.**
    Calm as slack water, twice as cold; buys anything, asks nothing,
    pays clean coin.
    She is what the Given Age's smuggling look like in the Lean Years,
    and whether her trade and the caves' older traffic are one
    operation is a question the canon holds open on purpose.
12. **Bett Harrow, the felting-mistress** *(new with the wool-walk)*.
    Broad, steam-reddened, cheerful in the way of people whose work is
    warm in winter; runs the fulling-shed, the island's second-biggest
    stove-circle and its women's parliament.
    Every year she judges the winter's felt, and her *tough, not hard*
    is the island's quality mark on cloth and character alike.

With them stand **Reef Halyard, the reef-pilot** — last of the trade
that knows the passes by feel, aground ashore all winter and worth a
pilot's fee the moment the season turns — and **Meg Wray, the
wool-carder**, sharp-tongued keeper of the carding-combs, who grades
every fleece the valley sends down and remembers every short-weight
sack for a decade.

## The naming register

For every future author: Farstone names come off the working world, and
the register is strict.

- **Surnames** are one hard, salt-worn syllable or two, taken from
  rigging, hull, tool, bird, or weather: Drust, Oakes, Cawl, Vane,
  Tenn, Pell, Quill, Quist, Ross, Vey, Voss, Renn, Wray, Harrow,
  **Strake** (a hull plank), **Halyard** (the line that hoists the
  sail).
  The best names carry two honest readings and the island never
  chooses between them — Reef Halyard is rigging and fishing-bank both.
- **Given names** are short and worn smooth: Wenna, Bram, Nessa, Dax,
  Mara, Greer, Orla, Ivy, Bett, Meg, Coilin, Brannaugh, Edda, Senan.
- **Standing titles do the work of honorifics**: Old Pell, Old Senan,
  Sister Edda, Mother nobody — the island grants *Old* like a medal.
- **Places** are plain working compounds, definite article included:
  the Net Loft, the Wool Walk, the Salt Lantern, Fizzwater Quay, the
  Shingle Strand.
  Nothing Latin, nothing epic, nothing that a tired fisherman wouldn't
  say twice a day.
- **Boats and wrecks** take seabird and workaday names: the *Gannet*,
  the grey lugger.

## The winter, plainly

The Off-Season is the island's crucible and its loom.
The dark comes early, the fleet ties up, and Farstone contracts to its
stove-circles: the Saltloft taproom, the fulling-shed, the net-loft, the
library's coal stove, the chapel's tallow light.
Winter is when the island literally makes its felt and mends its nets —
when it manufactures the very materials of holding-together — and the
engine's campaign walks the player up the same six steps every islander
once climbed: find your feet, a trade in hand, a keener eye, a roof of
your own, a hand in the dispute, a pillar of the harbor.
(The campaign is code at
`lib/emsenn/services/561-group/services/engineering-services-section/services/computer-engineering-services-section/services/computer-systems-engineering-services-section/services/agent-computer-system-engineering-services-section/services/multi-agent-computer-system-engineering-services-section/services/multi-agent-computer-platform-engineering-services-section/apps/feltos/src/farstone-campaign.mjs`;
the proverb came first.)

Tempers are as short as the season; warmth is warmth *against* the
cold; and the island ends every winter the way felt comes off the
stocks — smaller, denser, and harder to tear apart.

## Open questions / gaps

Recorded honestly rather than invented:

- **Where the founders sailed from** is unrecorded; the bronze torc and
  the grey-iron blade suggest an old northern coast, and the canon
  leaves it there.
- **The Gannet's loss** — year, cargo, crew — appears in no surviving
  register; the wreck predates every living pilot.
- **The island's flocks**: wool is canon (the wool-walk, the fleeces
  Meg Wray grades) but which farms run sheep, and how many, is not in
  the biome record.
- **Whether Riba Voss's trade continues the sea-caves' older
  smuggling** or merely rents its geography is held open, deliberately.
- **Maraset's festivals** beyond Slackwater giving-customs are
  undescribed; if they are ever written, they belong to the tide-clock,
  not the calendar.
