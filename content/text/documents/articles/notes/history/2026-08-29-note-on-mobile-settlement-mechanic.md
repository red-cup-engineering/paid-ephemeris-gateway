## The mechanic

If relationality is the house math, the city builder's mechanics can be theory-native: seasonal
rounds, a buffalo economy, and **tiospaye (kinship band) as the mobile "city" unit — instead of a
fixed settlement.** In the session's own words: "a city builder where the settlement itself moves
through the plains."

This inverts the genre's usual premise. Zeus/Poseidon/Caesar-style city builders (and their
Impressions Games lineage, already cited in the sierra-lucasarts note) fix a settlement in place
and grow it; Dawn of Man softens this with seasonal and material pressure but still roots
progress to a site. A tiospaye-as-city-unit model instead makes the settlement's mobility itself
the object being managed — the band moves with the buffalo, the seasons, and the grass, and what
would normally be "city growth" becomes band cohesion, herd management, and route/timing
decisions across the plains.

## Relationship to the campaign decision

The sierra-lucasarts note documents that the campaign is Lakota, built from real scholarship
alongside the house's own Lakotaean-metaphysics material (treated as the same underlying
mathematics as its relationality and semiotics work), under the house's standing honest-absence
rule. This note adds the one piece that decision didn't carry forward: the mechanic that would
make the campaign theory-native rather than a reskinned walker-economy — the mobile-band premise
is what lets the game's central loop be Lakota economic and kinship structure directly, rather
than genre-standard fixed-settlement growth with Lakota art assets over it.
